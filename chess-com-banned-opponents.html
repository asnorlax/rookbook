Chess.com banned opponents: the cheaters you played, and the refunds you missed

You've played cheaters. Everyone has. Chess.com catches most of them eventually, closes the account, and usually gives back the rating you lost — quietly, weeks later, with nothing tying it to a game. It never hands you the list. Rookbook does.

What happens when your opponent gets banned

When Chess.com's fair-play system closes an account for cheating, it's marked closed for fair play violations. Two things follow that most players never connect:

  • Your rated losses to that account are usually reversed. The rating comes back automatically, as if those games had never counted.
  • You're rarely told which game it was. The refund lands as a small adjustment between two later games, days or weeks on, with no message naming the opponent — miss it that day and you never saw it.

The ban is good news — a loss undone — but it arrives invisibly. The more useful question, how often does this happen to me, has no answer inside Chess.com.

Why you can't see it yourself

Chess.com will tell you an account is closed if you open that one profile. What it won't do is join the two halves: your game history on one side, each opponent's current status on the other. No page says "here are the eleven opponents you played who were later banned, and your record against them." Building that by hand means opening old games one at a time — hundreds of clicks for a list nobody keeps.

The information exists — every closed account is public, and so is your game archive. It just isn't joined. A tool that holds both sides at once closes that gap in seconds.

How rookbook tracks it

Rookbook already has your game history. The one extra step is Look up opponents — it reads each opponent's current profile through Chess.com's public API, including their account status. When that comes back as closed for fair play violations, the opponent is flagged everywhere they appear:

  • A ⛔ mark next to their name in your game log, with the loss struck through — it probably didn't stand.
  • A Closed opps count on the log summary — click it to filter the log to just those games.
  • A plain-English read: how many accounts you played were later closed for cheating, and how many of those games you lost.

Because the check reads Chess.com's live status, an account banned this morning shows up this afternoon.

New bans surface themselves

Rookbook doesn't make you re-scan the list for an unfamiliar name. On your first look, existing closed opponents count as already seen. After that, any account closed since your last look is marked new — a badge on the count, a NEW tag on the row. A fresh ban announces itself, which usually means a refund just landed.

The honest limits

Three things worth stating plainly:

  • It needs the lookup. Nothing is flagged until you run Look up opponents — the status isn't in your game data, it has to be fetched. One click, and it's cached.
  • "Closed" isn't always cheating. Only closed for fair play violations means caught cheating. An account can close for other reasons — a self-deletion, another policy breach — and rookbook keeps those distinct rather than calling everyone a cheat.
  • It reports; it doesn't adjudicate. Rookbook reads Chess.com's verdict rather than making one — if Chess.com hasn't closed an account, rookbook won't flag it, however the game felt.

Common questions

How do I know if a chess.com opponent was banned? Their profile shows as closed — and if it was for cheating, the reason is fair play violations. There's no built-in list of all your banned opponents, so seeing them all at once needs a tool that checks each opponent's status against your game history.

Does chess.com refund rating lost to a banned player? Usually, for rated games. Closing an account for fair play violations commonly reverses the rated results against it, so your lost rating returns — automatically, and often well after the game, with nothing naming the opponent.

How do I see every cheater I've played? Run rookbook's opponent lookup. It flags every account since closed for fair play violations, counts them, shows your record against them, and marks any closed since your last look.
